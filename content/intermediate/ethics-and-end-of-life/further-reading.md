**The guidance on withdrawing treatment.**
HPCSA, *Guidelines for the Withholding and Withdrawing of Treatment*, Booklet 7, revised September 2025, from the HPCSA's [ethical guidelines](https://www.hpcsa.co.za/). Short and plainly written. Read it for how the profession thinks about the end of life, not for anything about donation, which it does not mention.

**The evidence on opt-out.**
📌 Rithalia A, et al., [Impact of presumed consent for organ donation on donation rates: a systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2628300/), *BMJ* 2009. The most cited review, and still the clearest statement of why the law alone does not explain the differences between countries.

📌 Noyes J, et al., [Short-term impact of introducing a soft opt-out organ donation system in Wales](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6500329/), *BMJ Open* 2019. What happened when a close comparator made the switch.

**A South African view.**
Etheredge HR, "Assessing Global Organ Donation Policies: Opt-In vs Opt-Out", *Risk Management and Healthcare Policy* 2021;14:1985-1998. Written from Wits Donald Gordon Medical Centre.

**The system's own assessment.**
The Southern African Transplantation Society's *Organ and Tissue Donation in South Africa: Creating a National Strategy Roadmap*. The report of a national workshop, candid about access, consent rates and data, and the source of the principle that donation should be financially neutral.
