- **Chapter 8 of the National Health Act 61 of 2003 is the governing law**, and much of the detail sits in the regulations made under it, chiefly GN R180 of 2 March 2012. Cite the right one.

- **Separate the three kinds of statement:** legal requirement, clinical guidance and practice. Say which one you are giving.

- **Regulation 9 sets the two-doctor rule:** at least two doctors, one practising for five years or more since registration, and none of them may take part in the transplant. For eye tissue, an ordinary death certificate suffices. The "no interns" rule is not in the regulation.

- **Beyond the family's consent, two more authorisations can apply:** the hospital's medical practitioner in charge of clinical services, and, after an unnatural death, the Forensic Pathology Service. "Medical superintendent" and "district surgeon" are out of date.

- **Trade is a crime.** Section 60 makes it an offence to sell or trade in tissue, organs included, or for a donor to be rewarded beyond reasonable costs: up to five years' imprisonment.

- **A non-citizen or non-permanent-resident needs the Minister's written authorisation** to receive an organ (section 61(3)).

- **"It costs the family nothing" is practice, not statute.** Neither the Act nor the regulations allocate donation costs. There is also no legal duty to refer.

- **Two real recent developments:** the Ministerial Advisory Committee on Organ Transplantation (October 2024), and draft Regulations on Organ Transplantation (September 2026), which are **not yet law**.
