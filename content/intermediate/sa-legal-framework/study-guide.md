## The instruments

- **National Health Act 61 of 2003, Chapter 8.** Gazetted 23 July 2004. It repealed the Human Tissue Act 65 of 1983.
- **GN R180 of 2 March 2012.** *Regulations regarding the general control of human bodies, tissue, blood, blood products and gametes*, Government Gazette 35099. Where most of the operational detail sits.

## Definitions in section 1

- "Death" means brain death. Donation after circulatory death is practised under provincial policy and the national determination-of-death guidelines; a 2024 article in the *South African Journal of Bioethics and Law* argues it is lawful under Chapter 8.
- "Tissue" includes flesh, bone, a gland, an organ, skin, bone marrow or body fluid, but excludes blood and gametes.

## Regulation 9: establishment of death

- At least two medical practitioners.
- One must have practised for at least five years after registration.
- None may transplant the tissue removed from that person, or take part in the transplantation.
- Eye tissue: death is deemed established by the issuing of a death certificate by a medical practitioner.
- The claim that "neither may be an intern" does not appear in the regulation.

## Consent and authorisation

- **Consent:** the person's own donation, or the family in the order set by section 62 (Stage 2).
- **Hospital:** written authorisation from the medical practitioner in charge of clinical services, or a doctor they authorise. Not "medical superintendent", which is Human Tissue Act language.
- **Unnatural death:** a post-mortem is needed to determine the cause of death, and the Forensic Pathology Service must authorise what may be recovered. Not "district surgeon" or "state pathologist".

## Prohibitions

- **Section 60(4):** it is an offence for a donor to receive any financial or other reward, except reimbursement of reasonable costs incurred to provide the donation, and an offence to sell or trade in tissue except as Chapter 8 provides.
- **Section 60(5):** a fine, imprisonment of up to five years, or both.
- **Section 61(3):** no organ may be transplanted into a person who is not a South African citizen or permanent resident without the Minister's written authorisation.
- **Section 61(1)-(2):** organs from deceased persons may only be used, and must be allocated, as prescribed.

## What the law does not say

- **Costs.** Neither the Act nor the regulations allocate the costs of donation. The bill-free position is the practice stated by the SATCS Red File, the Organ Donor Foundation and the tissue banks. The family remains responsible for care given before death.
- **Referral.** There is no legal duty to refer a potential donor.

## Recent developments

**Ministerial Advisory Committee on Organ Transplantation.** Established by Government Notice 5360, Government Gazette 51352, 4 October 2024, under section 91 of the Act.

- Mandate: to advise the Minister on all matters related to organ transplantation.
- Functions include: the need for transplant facilities; advice on transplants involving unrelated donors, recipients and non-South African citizens; proposing which units may perform transplantation; monitoring unethical behaviour; working on the cost of transplantation; appraising transplant units' annual reports.
- Members: one Department of Health representative, one bioethicist and seven nephrologists, for five-year terms.

**Draft Regulations on Organ Transplantation.** Government Notice 7879, Government Gazette 55299, 4 September 2026, published for comment within three months. Not in force. As drafted, they would:

- require hospitals performing donation to designate staff who identify and refer potential donors (draft reg 3(4));
- require registers of every referral, donation and transplant, and regular reports to the national department (draft regs 6, 8 and 15);
- license transplant units (draft reg 9);
- require the Minister's approval for living donors not biologically related to the recipient (draft regs 10-11);
- require whoever removes organs from a deceased donor to reconstruct the body's appearance (draft reg 14);
- bar allocation of deceased-donor organs on the basis of race, religion, culture, language, gender, sexual orientation, disability or any other aspect of the donor's life with no bearing on the organ (draft reg 15(1));
- prohibit anyone offering a reward to a donor except as section 60 allows (draft reg 19).

**The 2025 amendment that is not a donation development.** The March 2025 amendment to regulations under the Act changed the regulations on the surveillance and control of notifiable medical conditions (Government Notice 6064, Government Gazette 52391, 27 March 2025). It has nothing to do with donation.
