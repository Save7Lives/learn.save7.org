Beginner Level gave you the short answer to the question people fear most: death is certified by at least two doctors, and none of them may have anything to do with the transplant. That answer is true, and for a conversation over coffee it is enough.

This Stage is for the next question, the one that comes when someone is really listening:

*How do they actually know?*

A family sitting beside a ventilated patient sees a warm body, a chest that rises and falls, and a heart beating on the monitor. Being told that this person has died contradicts everything their eyes are telling them. That is not ignorance. It is a reasonable response to an unusual situation, and it is one of the biggest obstacles anywhere in the donation pathway.

So this Stage explains how death is determined, at the level of *why* rather than *how to*. It covers both of the ways South Africa recognises: death by neurological criteria (brain death) and death by circulatory criteria. Each makes more sense next to the other, so they are taught together. The Stage then follows a donation from the moment someone first thinks of it to the moment organs and tissue are recovered, and names the organisations involved along the way.

One boundary holds throughout. **Nothing here is a procedure.** You will learn what the tests are and why they prove what they prove. You will not learn to perform them, and no clinical values are taught. The aim is to be able to explain, accurately, why a death is final.

Start from the definition, which is more precise than most people expect:

> Death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness together with the irreversible loss of the capacity to breathe.

It does not mention the heart. Keep that in mind through everything that follows.
