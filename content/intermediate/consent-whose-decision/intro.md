*Can my family override my wishes? If I register, is that the end of it? Who actually gets asked?*

These are the questions people put to advocates most often, and they are the ones most often answered wrongly. The usual mistake is to give only half the answer: either the law or what happens in practice, when the honest answer needs both.

South Africa is an **opt-in** country. Nobody is presumed to be a donor. Donation happens only when someone has positively decided to give: the person themselves while alive, or their family after death. So the question "whose decision is it?" sits at the centre of every donation.

This Stage answers it in three parts:

- **What the law says** about who may consent, and in what order.
- **What registering with the Organ Donor Foundation does**, and what it does not do.
- **How the family conversation is meant to happen**: separated from the news of death, unhurried, and free of pressure. Along the way you will see how the medical profession's general rules on informed consent apply to a decision no textbook consent form was written for.

By the end you should be able to explain, accurately and in plain words, who has to consent to a donation and why the most useful thing a person can do is tell their family.
