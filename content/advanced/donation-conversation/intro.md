A family is asked about donation on the worst day of their lives, sometimes in a language that isn't their first, and often without knowing what their relative would have wanted. How that conversation is conducted shapes what they decide, and whether they can live with the decision afterwards.

South Africa has its own structured approach to it: **Wits Transplant FACTS**, the *Family Approach to Consent for Transplant Strategy*. It was developed at Wits Transplant in Johannesburg and adapted from the UK's NHS Blood and Transplant guidance for South African hospitals, and SATCS reproduces it in the Red File. This Stage walks through its eight steps, its Do's and Don'ts, the two situations its authors say go wrong most often, and the *donor pause*.

**Know where your part begins and ends.** Some of what follows falls to anyone who is near a family at the end of a life: the words to avoid, the order things happen in, the reason donation is never mentioned before the family accepts the death. You will be expected to apply those. **Asking for consent is not one of them.** It belongs to the transplant coordinator working with the treating team, and completing this course qualifies nobody to do it.
