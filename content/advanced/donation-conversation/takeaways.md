- **FACTS is South Africa's own structured approach to the family conversation.** It comes from Wits Transplant, adapts the UK's NHS Blood and Transplant guidance, and has eight steps.

- **The eight steps:** planning, breaking bad news, a time-out break, assessing understanding and acceptance, the consent conversation, a second time-out break, the final family discussion, and follow-up.

- **The death and the donation are separate conversations.** The treating doctor gives the news of death. Donation is raised only after every key family member has understood and accepted the death, and never in the same meeting.

- **"Brain death is death."** Never say "life support" or that machines are keeping someone alive. When a family says it, correct it gently at once.

- **Donation is offered, not pushed.** There is no wrong answer. No negative, apologetic, manipulative or coercive language, and no waiting in the room for a reply.

- **A first "no" may be explored; a considered "no" is respected.** Exploring a refusal means checking it is informed, not wearing it down.

- **Families should not have to decide to switch off the machine.** Their relative has already died, and the machines will be switched off because of that, not because of anything the family decides.

- **The donor pause honours the donor.** A silent prayer or contemplation, usually in ICU with the family invited and again in theatre.

- **📌 Delay is not refusal.** In one small Korean study, families who took 48 hours or more consented at least as often as faster ones. Time with support is right; being rushed, or being left alone to decide, is not.

- **Your part:** refer early, keep donation out of the conversation until the family accepts the death, avoid misleading words, and keep supporting the family. Asking for consent is the coordinator's job, not yours.
