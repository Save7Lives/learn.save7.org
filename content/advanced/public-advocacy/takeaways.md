kind: TAKEAWAYS
stage: public-advocacy

---

- **No community is excluded from being offered donation.** Every potential donor is referred and every family is offered the conversation, whatever their background.

- **The assumption is the inequity.** Deciding that a family "wouldn't want to be asked" because of their culture, religion or ethnicity takes their decision away before they are given it. Bring faith support and interpreters instead.

- **Regulation 24 binds everyone.** No one may publish any fact that could possibly identify a donor, the person who consented, or a recipient, without consent. For recipients, that consent must be in writing.

- **Details identify as surely as names.** The test is whether someone *could* be identified, not whether they were.

- **Hospitals promote donation and protect people.** Awareness work is welcome. Donor details are not publicised, and a family may choose to go public itself.

- **Wait before telling a donation story.** Time keeps publicity away from acute grief and from a transplant that may still fail.

- **ULUNTU targets the barriers to consent, not just registration.** In the ODF's descriptions: culturally similar messengers, community settings, work with transplant coordinators, starting from a donor pool that does not represent the country.

- **Know what isn't known.** No evaluation of ULUNTU is available, and its current status couldn't be confirmed. Don't claim results for it.

- **Good advocacy leaves people informed and free to decide.** Judge it by that, not by how many say yes.
