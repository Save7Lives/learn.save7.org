## The roles

**Transplant coordinator.** A specialist nurse in organ donation (SNOD). The Wits Transplant handbook calls the role *procurement coordinator*. They screen referrals, plan the approach to the family, lead the donation conversation, secure authorisations, attend the recovery, support the family afterwards and audit outcomes.

**Tissue donation coordinator.** Handles tissue-only donation, has specialist communication training, and is generally on call around the clock. Takes over tissue recovery after the transplant coordinator has obtained consent from an organ donor's family.

**Donation support physician.** Additional critical care expertise a team can call in to help manage a consented donor.

📌 **Donation Specialist Nurse.** The Australian name for the equivalent of a transplant coordinator. Useful for reading Australian material, including the best-practice guideline this Level cites; it is not a South African title.

## The timing rule

The first discussion with the transplant coordinator takes place **before** end-of-life care is raised with the family. The reasons:

- donation is never raised where it turns out not to be feasible
- unsuitable referrals are screened out first
- the national priority list can be checked for patients who would accept organs others would decline
- the Organ Donor Foundation register can be checked

Treatment is not withdrawn or limited until the donation question has been clarified by, or with, the coordinator. When referring, nobody says "organ donation" to the family.

## The three referral triggers

In a patient on a ventilator, call the transplant coordinator when:

1. a decision has been made to perform brain death tests
2. there is a catastrophic brain injury with a Glasgow Coma Scale of 4 or less, not explained by sedation
3. the team intends to discuss withdrawing life-sustaining treatment, with death expected to follow

For tissue: most people who die are potential tissue donors, and the Red File asks that every death be referred to a tissue donation coordinator.

## The donor pathway

**Possible donor** (identified and referred by the treating team) → **potential donor** (assessed by the transplant team) → **eligible donor** (consented after a planned approach, by the treating and transplant teams together) → **actual donor** (recovery undertaken) → **utilised donor** (at least one organ transplanted).

The dead donor rule applies throughout: a patient becomes a donor only after death, and recovery must never cause death.

## The six duties

1. **Screening.** Feasibility, the register, the priority list.
2. **Planning.** Who is in the room, who says what, when, where and in which language.
3. **The conversation.** The treating team gives the diagnosis and prognosis. The coordinator raises donation and, wherever possible, obtains consent.
4. **Authorisation and logistics.** Forensic Pathology Service authorisation where a forensic post-mortem applies. Written authorisation for recovery from the medical practitioner in charge of clinical services (the Western Cape policy's *medical manager*). Coordinating theatre, where organ recovery takes priority over elective work. Present throughout the recovery.
5. **Aftercare.** Viewing after donation if the family wants it. Updates on which organs were used. Anonymous thank-you letters and bereavement support. Support for families who declined as well.
6. **Audit.** Feedback to referring staff after every referral. Every missed potential organ donor audited at morbidity and mortality meetings. A debrief after every donation after circulatory death.

## Who is responsible for what

- **Diagnosis, prognosis and the news of death:** the primary treatment team.
- **Raising donation and obtaining consent:** the transplant coordinator, with the treating team present at the start.
- **Donor management:** the treating team, except for a consented brain-dead donor, whose management the transplant team may legally take over.
- **Referral:** the whole team, as part of end-of-life care. No law requires it; professional practice does.

## SATCS

The **South African Transplant Coordinators Society**, founded on 30 June 2017 as a special interest group of the Southern African Transplantation Society. It works to a Code of Conduct, exists to educate, develop and support transplant coordinators, and produced the Organ and Tissue Donation Reference File (the Red File) for emergency departments and ICUs.

It is a voluntary professional society. It does not license or register anyone. Its Code of Conduct binds members to the codes of the Nursing Act and the HPCSA. Coordinators are usually experienced nurses, and nurses are registered and regulated by the **South African Nursing Council** under the Nursing Act 33 of 2005.
