- **A transplant coordinator is a specialist nurse in organ donation (a SNOD).** They lead the donor side of the process from referral to aftercare. Tissue-only donation has its own coordinator.

- **They are called before the family is told, not after.** The first discussion with the coordinator happens before end-of-life care is raised with the family, so that donation is never mentioned where it isn't feasible and the register and priority list have been checked.

- **Three triggers prompt the call:** a decision to test for brain death, a catastrophic brain injury with deep unconsciousness not explained by sedation, or an intention to discuss withdrawing life-sustaining treatment.

- **Referral is a professional duty, not a legal one, and it belongs to the whole team.** That includes nurses and ancillary staff, not only the treating doctor. Nobody mentions donation to the family while referring.

- **The duty chain has six parts:** screening, planning, the conversation, authorisation and logistics, aftercare, and audit. The last three are the ones people forget.

- **Family consent is necessary but not sufficient.** Forensic Pathology Service authorisation where a forensic post-mortem applies, and the medical manager's authorisation, come after it. The coordinator secures both.

- **The treating team gives the diagnosis; the coordinator raises donation.** Keeping those two jobs separate is what the next Stage is built on.

- **Every missed potential organ donor is audited.** Learning from the donations that didn't happen is part of the role, not an optional extra.

- **SATCS is the coordinators' professional society.** It was founded in 2017 under SATS, works to a Code of Conduct, and produced the Red File. It is not a regulator.
