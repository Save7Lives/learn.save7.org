Intermediate Level taught the rules: who may consent to donation, in what order, and what the National Health Act requires and forbids. This Stage asks a harder question. **What makes a family's consent actually valid?** It is not enough that the right person signed.

The answer comes from two places that are usually taught apart. The first is the ethics of consent that every South African health practitioner works under: the HPCSA's guidance on informed consent, capacity, decisions made on someone else's behalf, advance directives and the *best interests* test. The second is Chapter 8 of the National Health Act, read not as a list of rules but as it plays out in a real conversation with a real family.

Put side by side, they reveal something most people miss. **The decisions a family makes while their relative is dying and the decision they make about donation after death are different decisions.** They are made at different moments, by rules that differ, and sometimes by different people. Much of what goes wrong in consent, and much of what the FACTS steps in Stage 2 are designed to prevent, lives in the gap between the two.

This Stage explains the law and professional guidance. It is not legal advice.
