# POPIA Research Findings — Accounts, Login & Dashboard Spec

Grounds the account layer (student signup/login, student progress dashboard, Save7-staff admin oversight of individual students) against South Africa's **Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA)**, primary sources only — the Act itself and Information Regulator (South Africa) guidance — matching the standard already set for the National Health Act 61/2003 in the curriculum map (see `wayfinder/research/T02-intermediate-findings.md` and CONTEXT.md's "Source Note" concept). Feeds the downstream "Define student progress data model" ticket. Per CONTEXT.md, this ticket answers the legal-grounding question only; it does not itself design the data model, the signup flow, or Nerve Center.

## Method notes

- Primary Act text read directly and in full for the sections in scope (ss.1-38): **Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013**, retrieved from the Department of Justice & Constitutional Development's own legislation page, justice.gov.za/legislation/acts/2013-004.pdf (35 pp., consolidated text reflecting the commencement schedule up to GN 297/GG 44383 as amended — see Currency note below). Quoted/paraphrased directly from this text throughout; section numbers cited inline.
- Two Information Regulator (South Africa) primary documents read in full: the **Guidance Note on Processing of Special Personal Information** (June 2021, inforegulator.org.za) and the **Guidelines on Completing Section 22 Security Compromise Notification Form** (inforegulator.org.za). Both are official Regulator publications, not secondary commentary.
- No law-firm blog, "POPIA explainer" site, or other secondary summary was used as a source for any claim below. Where a claim is this research's own inference from the Act's text rather than something the Act or Regulator states explicitly, it is flagged as such (matching T01/T02's practice of distinguishing primary-sourced fact from inference).
- **Currency check:** the Act's founding provisions (Chapter 1 definitions, Chapter 3 conditions ss.8-35) commenced 1 July 2020 per the Act's own commencement table (s.115). The only later commencement item in the table is s.58(2) (prior-authorisation, unrelated to this ticket's scope), brought into force 1 February 2022 via GN 297/GG 44383 (1 April 2021), as amended by GN 560/GG 44761 (25 June 2021) and GenN 395/GG 44782 (30 June 2021). No amendment to ss.1-35 (definitions, the eight conditions, special personal information, children) was found in this consolidated text — treat the analysis below as current as of this research date (2026-09-16).

## 1. What counts as "personal information," and is any of it "special personal information"?

**Personal information (s.1):** POPIA defines "personal information" as "information relating to an identifiable, living, natural person," including (non-exhaustively) — quoting the operative paragraphs:
- (a) "information relating to the race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, national, ethnic or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, physical or mental health, well-being, disability, religion, conscience, belief, culture, language and birth of the person";
- (b) "information relating to the **education** or the medical, financial, criminal or employment history of the person" (emphasis added);
- (c) "any identifying number, symbol, e-mail address, physical address, telephone number, location information, online identifier or other particular assignment to the person";
- (d) biometric information; (e) personal opinions/views/preferences; (f) private correspondence; (g) another individual's views about the person; (h) the person's name, "if it appears with other personal information relating to the person or if the disclosure of the name itself would reveal information about the person."

Applying this directly to the account layer's data:
- **Account/profile data** (name, email, any identifying number) falls squarely under (c) and (h).
- **Baseline Sitting quiz scores and Stage/Level/Certificate completion state** fall squarely under **(b) — "information relating to the education... history of the person."** A student's assessment results and completion record are, definitionally, part of their "education history." This is a direct textual hit, not an inference.

**Special personal information (s.26):** POPIA defines this narrowly — "special personal information" *means* personal information referred to in s.26, i.e.:
- (a) "the religious or philosophical beliefs, race or ethnic origin, trade union membership, political persuasion, **health** or sex life or biometric information of a data subject"; or
- (b) criminal behaviour (alleged offences / proceedings).

**On the specific question of whether Baseline Sitting scores are "special personal information" because the course is about organ donation:** the answer, grounded directly in the Act's own wording, is **no**. Both s.1(a) ("physical or mental health... of the person") and s.26(a) ("health... of a data subject") define the special category by reference to the data subject's *own* health status — not to a topic a data subject has been assessed on. A quiz score measuring a student's *comprehension of facts about organ donation* is not information about that student's own health; it is ordinary "education history" personal information under s.1(b). This is this research's own reasoned application of the Act's text to the facts (the Information Regulator's Special Personal Information Guidance Note, read in full for this ticket, does not address this specific scenario and offers no disambiguation between "health information" and "information about a health-adjacent educational topic" — flagged as an inference, not a directly-stated rule, though a fairly unambiguous one given how narrowly both definitions are worded around the data subject's own condition).
- **Consequence:** none of the described data (account/profile, quiz scores, completion state) currently triggers s.26's prohibition or the ss.27-33 exception/authorisation machinery (which otherwise requires, per the Information Regulator's June 2021 Guidance Note §4.1, either a s.27(1) exception or a Regulator authorisation under s.27(2) "in the public interest" with "appropriate safeguards"). **If** the platform later adds a field capturing a student's own health status, disability, religion, etc. (none currently planned), that field would immediately become special personal information and require one of the ss.27-33 gateways.
- **Flag for the data model (children):** separately from special personal information, POPIA Part C (ss.34-35) imposes an equivalent prohibition-plus-exception structure specifically for **personal information of a child** ("a natural person under the age of 18 years who is not legally competent... without the assistance of a competent person," s.1). Processing a child's personal information is prohibited (s.34) unless carried out with a competent person's prior consent or the Regulator has authorised it (s.35(1)-(2)). Neither the ticket nor CONTEXT.md specifies a minimum signup age for learn.save7.org; if under-18 students can sign up, this is a live, separate compliance gate the data model needs — see §6.

## 2. Lawful basis for collecting this data at signup, and consent/notice obligations

**Lawful processing basis — s.11(1):** personal information may only be processed if one of six conditions is met: (a) the data subject (or competent person, if a child) consents; (b) processing is necessary to conclude or perform a contract to which the data subject is party; (c) it complies with a legal obligation on the responsible party; (d) it protects a legitimate interest of the data subject; (e) it is necessary for a public body's public-law duty; or (f) it is necessary for the responsible party's or a third party's legitimate interests.

For a general-public signup flow with no existing relationship, **s.11(1)(a) — consent** is the cleanest and most defensible basis, and squares with s.1's definition of consent as "any voluntary, specific and informed expression of will in terms of which permission is given for the processing of personal information." (s.11(1)(b), necessity for contract performance, could arguably also apply once a student accepts terms of service to use the Course, but consent is the more conservative basis for a first-party signup collecting profile and assessment data, and is required in any event for anything the platform later wants to do with the data beyond the original purpose — see §4.) Two consent-specific rules follow directly from s.11(2): the responsible party (Save7) **bears the burden of proof** of consent, and the data subject may **withdraw consent at any time**, without affecting the lawfulness of processing that already occurred under that consent.

**Notice at collection — s.18 ("Condition 6: Openness"):** if personal information is collected, the responsible party "must take reasonably practicable steps to ensure that the data subject is aware of" — per s.18(1)(a)-(h) — the information being collected and its source; the responsible party's name and address; the purpose of collection; whether supply is voluntary or mandatory and the consequences of not providing it; any law authorising/requiring the collection; whether the information will be transferred to a third country and its protection level there; and, "any further information such as the-(i) recipient or category of recipients of the information; (ii) nature or category of the information; (iii) existence of the right of access to and the right to rectify the information collected; (iv) existence of the right to object to the processing... as referred to in section 11(3); and (v) right to lodge a complaint to the Information Regulator and the contact details of the Information Regulator" — "which is necessary, having regard to the specific circumstances in which the information is or is not to be processed, to enable processing in respect of the data subject to be reasonable." These steps must be taken **before** collection, where information is collected directly from the data subject (s.18(2)(a)) — i.e., before or at signup, not after.

**Practical implication for the signup flow's data model:** the signup-time notice (privacy notice / consent screen) should name, per s.18(1)(h)(i), the categories of recipients of the data — including, explicitly, that a future completion signal may be shared with a separate Save7-affiliated system (see §4 for why naming this now, even though Nerve Center's own design is out of scope, materially simplifies that later step). Two further Chapter 3 conditions apply irrespective of the s.11 basis chosen: **s.9** (processing must be lawful and not infringe privacy — "Condition 2: Processing limitation") and **s.10** (minimality — personal information "may only be processed if, given the purpose for which it is processed, it is adequate, relevant and not excessive"), which argues for the signup form collecting only what the progress-dashboard and certificate functions actually need.

## 3. Obligations on admin accounts viewing individual students' data

Several distinct Chapter 3 conditions bear on this, plus one definitional question (operator vs. responsible party) that changes which sections apply.

- **Purpose specification (s.13) — "Condition 3":** "Personal information must be collected for a specific, explicitly defined and lawful purpose related to a function or activity of the responsible party" (s.13(1)). Save7's admin-oversight function needs its own explicitly defined purpose (e.g., "administering student accounts, tracking Level/Stage/Certificate progress, and supporting individual students") distinct from, say, a hypothetical future marketing purpose — an admin dashboard built to serve only the defined purpose is what s.13 requires.
- **Further processing limitation (s.15) — "Condition 4":** "Further processing of personal information must be in accordance or compatible with the purpose of collection" (s.15(1)). If admin staff want to use student data for anything beyond the purpose named at signup, s.15(2) sets out the compatibility factors to weigh: "(a) the relationship between the purpose of the intended further processing and the purpose for which the information has been collected; (b) the nature of the information concerned; (c) the consequences of the intended further processing for the data subject; (d) the manner in which the information has been collected; and (e) any contractual rights and obligations between the parties." Individual-student oversight for progress/support purposes is squarely compatible with the original educational purpose; a materially different secondary use (e.g., admin using student data to build promotional case studies without separate consent) would need to be tested against these factors or obtain fresh consent under s.15(3)(a).
- **Data minimisation (s.10):** applies to admin views the same way it applies to collection — an admin dashboard should expose only the fields the oversight function needs (e.g., completion state and a Baseline trend), not an unfiltered dump of every stored field, if the broader fields aren't "adequate, relevant and not excessive" for that specific admin task.
- **Accountability (s.8) — "Condition 1":** "The responsible party must ensure that the conditions set out in this Chapter, and all the measures that give effect to such conditions, are complied with **at the time of the determination of the purpose and means of the processing and during the processing itself**." This places the compliance burden on Save7 as the institution (the responsible party), not on individual admin staff members personally — but Save7 must design the admin tooling (role scoping, field-level access, audit logging) to actually enforce the above conditions, not just declare them.
- **Operator vs. responsible party (s.1, ss.20-21):** this distinction is directly relevant to *how Save7 staff relate to the data*, and the answer is that **admin staff are not "operators."** POPIA defines "operator" as "a person who processes personal information **for** a responsible party **in terms of a contract or mandate, without coming under the direct authority of that party**" (s.1) — i.e., an external third party (e.g., an outsourced hosting or analytics vendor) processing on Save7's instructions. Save7 staff/program-coordinator admin accounts act *under Save7's own direct authority* as part of the responsible party itself; they are not a separate "operator." Practically, this means:
  - The operator-specific obligations in **s.20** ("An operator or anyone processing personal information on behalf of a responsible party or an operator, must (a) process such information only with the knowledge or authorisation of the responsible party; and (b) treat personal information which comes to their knowledge as confidential...") and **s.21** (written contract requiring the operator to maintain s.19 security measures, and to "notify the responsible party immediately where there are reasonable grounds to believe that the personal information of a data subject has been accessed or acquired by any unauthorised person") describe the shape of the relationship Save7 would need with any *external* processor (e.g., a hosting provider), **not** the internal admin-staff relationship.
  - Internal admin access is instead governed directly by **s.19** (security safeguards — the responsible party itself must secure the integrity and confidentiality of the data, including against its own staff's unlawful access) and by the purpose/further-processing/minimality conditions above. In data-model terms: build role-based access control and purpose-scoped views as the s.19/s.13 compliance mechanism for admin accounts, and reserve a written s.21 contract with security-safeguard and breach-notification-to-Save7 clauses for any *external* vendor the platform later brings on (e.g., cloud hosting, a third-party analytics tool) — that is a distinct, additional obligation that doesn't apply to Save7's own staff.

## 4. Constraints on a later cross-reference/export of completion status to a separate platform

This is squarely a **"further processing" (s.15)** question, plus a question of whether the receiving platform ("Save7 OS / Nerve Center," per the map's out-of-scope note) becomes its own **"responsible party"** for the data it receives.

- **Is Nerve Center a new responsible party, or an operator?** POPIA's s.1 definitions turn this on *who determines the purpose and means*. "Responsible party" means "a public or private body or any other person which, alone or in conjunction with others, **determines the purpose of and means for processing personal information**"; "operator" is someone who processes *for* a responsible party *without* determining that purpose themselves, under contract/mandate. Nerve Center is described (per the map issue #13) as "a separate, future platform reserved for Save7 volunteers" that will use a completion signal to **authorise its own accounts** — i.e., it will determine its *own* purpose (volunteer-account eligibility) and its *own* means for that determination. On the facts as scoped, that makes Nerve Center a **new, independent responsible party** for the data it receives, not merely an operator processing on Learn's instructions. (If Save7 instead designed Nerve Center to act strictly under Learn's instruction for Learn's own purpose — which the map's framing does not suggest — the operator analysis in §3 would apply instead. This is this research's inference from the Act's definitional test applied to the map's own description of Nerve Center, not a directly-stated Regulator ruling on this specific scenario.)
- **Does this need fresh consent, or does the original notice/consent cover it?** Per s.15(1), "further processing of personal information must be in accordance or compatible with the purpose of collection," assessed (s.15(2)) against: the relationship between the original and new purpose; the nature of the information; the consequences for the data subject; the manner of collection; and contractual rights/obligations. Per s.15(3), further processing is **not** incompatible if, among other grounds, "the data subject or a competent person where the data subject is a child has consented to the further processing of the information" (s.15(3)(a)). Practically:
  - If the s.18 notice given **at signup** already discloses, per s.18(1)(h)(i), that a completion/eligibility signal may in future be shared with a Save7-affiliated volunteer platform, and the student's original s.11(1)(a) consent covers that disclosure, the original consent/notice **can** cover the later export — no fresh consent event is needed at export time. This is the strongest argument for naming the future Nerve Center handoff in the signup-time notice now, even though Nerve Center's own design is out of scope for this ticket.
  - If the original notice/consent is silent on this recipient, the export is a new purpose requiring either a fresh s.15(3)(a) consent from the student at that time, or a case-by-case s.15(2) compatibility assessment — a materially weaker position, since s.15(2)(c) ("consequences of the intended further processing for the data subject") is harder to justify retroactively for a use the student was never told about.
  - Separately, because Nerve Center would be a new responsible party in its own right, **it** would independently need its own lawful basis (s.11) and its own s.18 notice to the data subject for *its* processing of the received signal — Learn's compliance does not automatically satisfy Nerve Center's own obligations as a distinct responsible party. That is a Nerve Center-map concern, not this ticket's, but the data model this ticket feeds should be built so the *export itself* is defensible on Learn's side regardless of what Nerve Center later does.
- **Minimisation as a further-processing safeguard:** s.10 and the s.15(2)(b)-(c) factors both point the same way — the data model should export the **minimum signal** (e.g., a binary/enum "eligible" flag per Level, or "completed all three Levels: yes/no") rather than raw Baseline Sitting scores or full quiz histories, since a narrower export is both easier to justify as "compatible" under s.15(2) and inherently more minimal under s.10.
- No transborder-flow issue (Chapter 9, s.72) arises unless Nerve Center is hosted or operated outside the Republic — not indicated by the map.

## 5. Retention, security safeguards, and breach notification

**Retention (s.14) — "Condition 3, cont'd":** "records of personal information must not be retained any longer than is necessary for achieving the purpose for which the information was collected or subsequently processed" (s.14(1)), **unless** — per s.14(1)(a)-(d) — retention is required/authorised by law, "reasonably requires the record for lawful purposes related to its functions or activities," is required by a contract between the parties, or the data subject (or competent person, for a child) has consented to the retention. s.14(2) separately permits retention beyond the ordinary limit "for historical, statistical or research purposes if the responsible party has established appropriate safeguards against the records being used for any other purposes." Once the responsible party is "no longer authorised to retain the record," s.14(4) requires it to "destroy or delete a record of personal information or de-identify it as soon as reasonably practicable," and s.14(5) requires that destruction/deletion be done "in a manner that prevents its reconstruction in an intelligible form."
- **Applied to Baseline Sitting history:** per CONTEXT.md, the whole design point of the four fixed Baseline Sittings (initial + after each Level) is the **before/after comparison across sittings** — so retaining a student's full Baseline history for the duration of their active enrolment across all three Levels is squarely "reasonably required for lawful purposes related to [Save7's] functions" under s.14(1)(b). What the Act does **not** support is *indefinite* retention once that purpose is exhausted (e.g., long after a student finishes or abandons the Course): the data model should set a defined retention window (e.g., tied to account inactivity or a fixed period after Certificate issuance / account closure) with a scheduled deletion or de-identification path, both to satisfy s.14(1)/(4)-(5) and to support a student's s.24 deletion-request right.

**Security safeguards (s.19) — "Condition 7":** the responsible party "must secure the integrity and confidentiality of personal information in its possession or under its control by taking appropriate, reasonable technical and organisational measures to prevent (a) loss of, damage to or unauthorised destruction of personal information; and (b) unlawful access to or processing of personal information" (s.19(1)). s.19(2) requires "reasonable measures to (a) identify all reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks... (b) establish and maintain appropriate safeguards against the risks identified; (c) regularly verify that the safeguards are effectively implemented; and (d) ensure that the safeguards are continually updated in response to new risks or deficiencies," with regard to "generally accepted information security practices" (s.19(3)). The Information Regulator's June 2021 Guidance Note on Special Personal Information (§4.3) restates this same four-part test (identify risks → establish safeguards → verify → update) as the operative standard even outside the special-personal-information context, confirming it as the Regulator's expected baseline security posture generally. For the data model, this argues for (at minimum) encryption of stored profile/assessment data, role-based/least-privilege admin access (tying back to §3), and a documented, periodically-reviewed risk assessment — rather than treating "security" as a one-time technical checkbox.

**Breach notification (s.22) — "Condition 8, adjacent":** triggered "where there are reasonable grounds to believe that the personal information of a data subject has been accessed or acquired by any unauthorised person" (s.22(1)). The responsible party must then notify **both** the Information Regulator **and** the affected data subject(s) (s.22(1)(a)-(b)), "unless the identity of such data subject cannot be established." Timing: "as soon as reasonably possible after the discovery of the compromise," subject only to a delay where a law-enforcement public body or the Regulator determines that notifying would impede a criminal investigation (s.22(2)-(3)). Data-subject notice must be in writing, via one of the channels listed in s.22(4) (post, email, prominent website placement, news media, or as directed by the Regulator), and must include, per s.22(5): a description of the possible consequences of the compromise; the measures the responsible party has taken or intends to take; a recommendation of steps the data subject can take to mitigate adverse effects; and, if known, the identity of the unauthorised person. The Information Regulator's own **Guidelines on Completing Section 22 Security Compromise Notification Form** add the operational detail that shapes what an incident-response data model needs to capture and produce quickly: the responsible party retains ultimate reporting responsibility even where an operator is involved (an operator must notify the responsible party "immediately" on discovering a compromise, per the Guidelines' step 5, mirroring s.21(2)); and the Regulator's own notification form requires — among other fields — the date of the compromise, date reported, an explanation for any delay, the *type* of security compromise, the *type* of personal information unlawfully accessed, the *number* of affected data subjects, the method used to notify them, and a description of both the possible consequences and the mitigation measures taken.
- **Practical trigger point for this platform:** given the account layer's fields (profile info, Baseline Sitting scores, completion state — none of it "special personal information" per §1), a breach exposing any of that data to an unauthorised party is enough to trigger s.22 — there is no minimum-severity or minimum-record-count threshold in the Act itself. The data model should therefore be built so that, per an incident, Save7 can quickly answer exactly the questions the Regulator's own form demands: which students were affected, which specific fields were exposed, and when the compromise occurred and was discovered — since s.22(2)'s "as soon as reasonably possible" clock starts at *discovery*, not at confirmation of full impact.

## 6. Flagged gaps / implications for the account & data model

- **G1 — Minor-student age gating is unresolved and legally load-bearing.** Neither this ticket, CONTEXT.md, nor the map (#13) states a minimum signup age. If under-18 students can create accounts, Part C of the Act (ss.34-35) imposes the same prohibition-plus-consent/authorisation structure on a child's personal information that s.26 imposes on special personal information (see §1) — meaning signup would need a "competent person's" (parent/guardian's) prior consent (s.35(1)(a)) rather than the student's own consent under s.11(1)(a). This should be resolved explicitly (age gate, or parental-consent flow) before the signup data model is finalised — it is a real POPIA gate this research did not need to invent, since s.34/35 already state it.
- **G2 — The "special personal information" conclusion in §1 is this research's textual inference, not a Regulator ruling on this exact fact pattern.** The Information Regulator's Special Personal Information Guidance Note (June 2021) was read in full and does not address "assessment scores on a health-adjacent topic" as a scenario. The conclusion that Baseline Sitting scores are not special personal information rests on a straightforward reading of ss.1(a) and 26(a) (both anchored to the data subject's *own* health), but if the platform's risk tolerance is low, it would be worth a direct written query to the Regulator (POPIACompliance@inforegulator.org.za, the address used for special-personal-information applications per the Guidance Note) before finalising the data model, rather than relying solely on this research's reading.
- **G3 — No primary source found specifically distinguishing "internal admin access" from "operator access" in Regulator guidance.** The operator/responsible-party analysis in §3 is built directly from the Act's own s.1 definitions (which are unambiguous on this point) rather than from a Regulator guidance note interpreting that boundary — no such note was found in this research pass. Flagged only because T02's precedent (NHA research) generally found a named guideline for each sub-question; here, the Act's text itself was sufficient and no additional Regulator commentary exists to cross-check against.
- **G4 — Nerve Center's own responsible-party status is this research's inference from the map's description**, not from anything Nerve Center itself has committed to design-wise (it is explicitly out of scope for this ticket and this map). If Nerve Center's eventual design instead has it acting strictly on Learn's instructions for Learn's own purpose, the s.20/21 operator analysis would apply instead of the new-responsible-party analysis in §4 — this should be re-checked once Nerve Center is actually scoped as its own map.
- **G5 — No concrete retention period is specified anywhere in the Act, CONTEXT.md, or the map.** s.14 sets the *test* (no longer than necessary for the purpose, subject to the s.14(1)(a)-(d) exceptions) but, correctly, does not prescribe a number of years — that judgment call belongs to the data-model ticket, informed by §5 above.

## Implications for learn.save7.org (concrete design constraints for issue #16)

Translating §1–§6 above into a punch list for the student progress data model / Nerve Center eligibility signal ticket:

1. **Data inventory is all "personal information," none currently "special personal information."** Name, contact details, Baseline Sitting scores (all 4 sittings), and Stage/Level/Certificate completion state are all POPIA "personal information" (s.1) — treat all of it as in-scope for every Chapter 3 condition from day one. None of it is "special personal information" under s.26 (§1 above), so the ss.27–33 authorisation machinery does not apply — **unless** the platform later adds a field capturing a student's own health, religion, race, trade-union membership, political persuasion, or criminal-behaviour data, at which point that specific field (not the rest of the model) would need a s.27 gateway.
2. **Resolve the minors question before finalising signup, don't leave it implicit.** Nothing in CONTEXT.md or the map sets a minimum signup age (G1 above). Pick one explicitly: (a) an 18+-only restriction stated in the spec, or (b) an age-capture step at signup that branches under-18 accounts into a **competent person's prior consent** flow (s.11(1)(a) and s.35(1)(a)) before any profile or Baseline Sitting data is collected. Either is compliant; leaving it unstated is not.
3. **The signup consent/notice screen must be an itemised s.18(1) checklist, not a generic "I agree to the Privacy Policy" checkbox.** It must state, at minimum: what's being collected and its source; Save7's identity/contact details as responsible party; the specific purpose ("to create your account, record your Baseline Assessment results and Level/Stage/Certificate progress"); which fields are mandatory vs. optional and the consequence of withholding them; whether data may be transferred to a third country (relevant if any hosting/Nerve Center infrastructure is non-South African — flag this possibility now even if unresolved, per s.18(1)(g)); the categories of recipients (naming that a future completion signal may be shared with a Save7-affiliated platform, per s.18(1)(h)(i), materially strengthens the later Nerve Center export — see point 6); and the rights to access/correct/object and to complain to the Information Regulator (s.18(1)(h)(iii)–(v)).
4. **Admin dashboards must be purpose-scoped and minimal by design, not just by policy.** Default coordinator/staff views to aggregate/cohort data; gate individual-student drill-down behind a named purpose tied to the s.13 purpose statement; implement role-based access control and field-level minimisation as the practical mechanism for discharging s.10 (minimality), s.13 (purpose specification), and s.19(2)(a) ("identify all reasonably foreseeable internal... risks," which includes over-broad staff access) — POPIA doesn't name RBAC specifically, but it's the standard way to satisfy these conditions and should be treated as a hard requirement for admin tooling.
5. **Set a stated, non-indefinite retention policy.** POPIA s.14 sets a purpose-bound test, not a fixed number of years, but "no stated policy" is itself non-compliant. Recommended concrete default: retain Baseline Sitting history and completion/Certificate records for the life of the active account (justified under s.14(1)(b) by the longitudinal 4-sitting design and by Certificates being a standing, re-downloadable credential), plus a defined post-closure window (e.g., long enough to support Certificate re-verification requests) — then destroy/de-identify per s.14(4)–(5). Pick and document an actual number rather than leaving retention open-ended.
6. **Before any data flows to Save7 OS/Nerve Center, document which POPIA relationship applies and update the signup notice accordingly.** Per §4's analysis, Nerve Center most plausibly becomes its own **responsible party** (it will determine its own purpose — volunteer-account eligibility — and its own means), which makes the export a s.15 further-processing event on Learn's side: it is compliant if the signup-time notice already discloses this recipient/purpose (point 3 above) and the original consent covers it; otherwise fresh consent or a case-by-case s.15(2) compatibility test is needed at export time. Export the **minimum viable signal** (e.g., a per-Level eligibility flag) rather than raw scores, both to stay within s.10 minimality and to keep the s.15(2) compatibility case easy to make. If Nerve Center's hosting/operating entity ends up outside South Africa, run the Chapter 9 s.72 transborder-transfer test (adequate-protection agreement, or data-subject consent, are the most workable grounds) before that export goes live — this was out of scope for Nerve Center's own map but is a live constraint on the export mechanism itself.
7. **Security and breach-notification duties to build into the data model, not bolt on later:** encryption in transit and at rest for all account/profile/assessment data; a documented, periodically-reviewed risk-identification process (s.19(2)); and an operational incident-response path that can answer, within the Regulator's own notification-form fields, exactly which students were affected, which fields were exposed, and the compromise/discovery dates — since the s.22(2) "as soon as reasonably possible" clock starts at discovery, not at confirmed full impact, and there is no minimum-severity threshold in the Act before s.22 is triggered.
