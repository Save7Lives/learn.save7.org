# T04 Video Review — `The_Journey_of_a_Gift.mp4`

Resolves the gap both Beginner (#2) and Advanced (#4) research flagged: neither research pass could watch or transcribe the video. The user watched it directly and supplied a full transcript, reproduced below, plus a content/tone/Stage read.

## Genre and tone

**Explainer**, not a donor-family testimonial or personal narrative. Friendly, direct-to-camera narration walking through "the journey" of a donation conceptually (need → brain death → legal framework → consent conversation → outcome), not a single real person's story. This corrects the premise floated in T01 (Beginner findings), which speculated it might anchor a standalone "human-story Stage" — there is no individual donor/recipient story here to anchor that.

## Content summary

Covers, in order:
1. **Scale of impact** — one donor can save 7+ lives via organs and transform 65+ via tissue donation (matches the "7 Lives" tagline already sourced from `7 Lives in 7 Steps.pdf`).
2. **The need** — end-stage organ failure, advanced lung disease (<50% 2-year survival), burn victims needing skin, corneal blindness.
3. **Brain death** — defined precisely (total, irreversible loss of brainstem function; not coma/vegetative state; no recorded recovery ever). Directly reinforces Beginner myth #1 (ventilator ≠ alive).
4. **Two donation pathways** — donation after brain death (DBD, most common) vs. donation after circulatory death (DCD, palliative withdrawal-of-care scenario).
5. **SA legal/ethical framework** — Chapter 8, National Health Act (NHA 61/2003): two independent doctors uninvolved in the transplant team must certify brain death (one ≥5 years' experience); a state pathologist signs off for accidental/unnatural deaths; treating and transplant teams are legally required to stay separate (conflict-of-interest safeguard).
6. **No cost to the family** — explicit, by law.
7. **The consent conversation** — a trained transplant coordinator approaches the family only after they've absorbed the death diagnosis; no pressure, no false hope.
8. **Next-of-kin primacy** — even a registered donor's wishes require family consent in South Africa; this is named as the single most important takeaway.
9. **Tissue-donation legacy** — corneas, bone, skin grafts.
10. **Call to action** — not "register," but "talk to your family."
