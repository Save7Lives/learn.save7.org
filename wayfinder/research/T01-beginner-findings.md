# T01 Beginner Level — Research Findings

Grounds the Beginner Level (awareness and myth-busting, general-public audience, South African context) against the Source Corpus and current South African primary sources (Organ Donor Foundation of South Africa — ODF — and the South African Transplant/Transplantation Society — SATS). Per CONTEXT.md, this feeds the ticket that finalizes the Beginner Level's module list and Stage breakdown; it does not itself decide Stages.

## Method notes

- Read in full: `7 Lives in 7 Steps.pdf` (2 pp.) and `Transplant Alchemy 101 - Study Guide.pdf` (4 pp.) — both short enough to read in one pass, no page range needed.
- `The_Journey_of_a_Gift.mp4` could not be watched or transcribed by this research pass — see Gap G1.
- ODF (odf.org.za) and SATS (sats.org.za) pages were fetched directly for primary-source content; news coverage (SAnews, allAfrica, Channel Africa, Spotlight) was used only to locate primary documents or to attribute named quotes, and is flagged as secondary wherever the underlying primary document could not be independently located.
- The most authoritative national statistics found are the SATS/South African Transplant Coordinators Society (SATCS) joint report to the WHO-ONT Global Observatory on Donation and Transplantation (GODT), **"5-Year National Organ Transplant Activity, South Africa 2017-2021"** (sats.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/5-Year-National-Organ-Transplant-Activity-3.pdf). This is the primary source cited throughout Section 2 unless noted otherwise.

## 1. Candidate SA-specific myths/misconceptions to debunk

Each entry cites the primary source it was found in.

1. **"If I'm a registered donor, doctors won't try as hard to save my life / will let me die sooner."**
   Source: *Transplant Alchemy 101 - Study Guide.pdf*, Objective 2 — "Misunderstandings regarding brain death — such as believing a patient is still 'alive' because a ventilator is providing oxygen — can lead families to refuse consent." Corroborated structurally by Objective 3 ("Two independent registered medical doctors, who are not part of the transplant team, are legally required to certify brain death") and Objective 4 (same two-doctor, non-transplant-team certification requirement, one doctor HPCSA-registered ≥5 years). ODF FAQ (odf.org.za/faqs/) confirms the same separation in general terms: the treating medical team is distinct from the transplant team.

2. **"Donation will disfigure my body / I can't have an open-casket funeral."**
   Sources: ODF FAQ (odf.org.za/faqs/) — "The recovery of organs and tissue is carried out with great care" and does not change the body's appearance; ODF Cornea FAQ (odf.org.za/cornea-faqs/) — "Great care is taken to preserve the appearance of the donor," open-casket viewing remains possible. Also named directly as a real-world refusal reason in *Transplant Alchemy 101*, Objective 2: "Refusals may stem from concerns about body disfigurement during recovery surgery, despite the fact that the body is restored to its original state afterward."

3. **"My family will be charged / my medical aid or estate will be billed for donation."**
   Sources: ODF FAQ — no cost to register or for the family; hospitals cover all medical expenses. *Transplant Alchemy 101*, Objective 4 — "By law, the donor's medical aid, estate, and next of kin are not responsible for any costs related to the donation after brain death has been declared and consent obtained" (citing Chapter 8, National Health Act No. 61 of 2003).

4. **"Organs get bought and sold / my family could be paid."**
   Source: ODF FAQ — donation is a voluntary gift; "trading organs is illegal." (General myth also flagged in secondary write-ups, e.g. Affinity Health, but the illegality claim is grounded directly in the ODF FAQ, a primary source.)

5. **"My religion forbids organ donation."**
   Source: ODF FAQ — "Most religions support organ and tissue donation as it is consistent with life preservation." *Transplant Alchemy 101*, Objective 2 groups "fear-based understandings, cultural barriers, myths, and misunderstandings within communities" as a named cause of lost donations, without itemising specific SA faith traditions — see Gap G2.

6. **"I'm too old / not healthy enough / wear glasses, so I can't donate."**
   Sources: ODF FAQ — "no strict upper age limit"; suitability is assessed at time of death. ODF Cornea FAQ — corneas "can be used even if a donor wears glasses" and poor eyesight/cataracts don't disqualify a donor. *Transplant Alchemy 101*, Objective 5 — "There is no age restriction for solid organ donation"; "Extended criteria donors with comorbidities like hypertension, diabetes, or HIV may still be eligible."

7. **"Registering as a donor is enough — my wishes will automatically be followed."**
   This is the inverse of a comforting myth and is one of the most consequential SA-specific points to correct: consent from next of kin is legally required regardless of registration status. Source: *Transplant Alchemy 101*, Objective 2 — "Organs may be lost because registered donors do not share their intentions with their loved ones, as consent from next of kin is always required for donation to proceed." Objective 4 confirms the legal requirement for next-of-kin/guardian informed consent.

8. **"If my family agreed once, donation will definitely go ahead."**
   The reverse problem: cultural/family objection at the bedside can override prior individual consent. Source (named, on-record SA government spokesperson, so treated as a citable primary quote rather than secondary commentary): Steve Mabona, Gauteng Department of Health spokesperson, quoted in Channel Africa (26 Aug 2026): "It's a challenge, because someone would have agreed, but then by the time we want to implement what was agreed upon, the family says culturally we don't believe in this."

9. **"South Africa's organ shortage isn't really that severe / donation rates are adequate."**
   Source: SATS/SATCS 5-Year National Organ Transplant Activity report — national deceased-donor rate fell from 1.60 per million population (pmp) in 2017 to 0.48 pmp in 2021 (see Section 2), against Spain's 47.05 pmp in 2017 (GODT data, cited via the Spotlight article referencing a 2020 South African Medical Journal study) — a stark comparison worth using to counter complacency.

10. **Tissue-donation-specific myths bundled with organ myths** (worth separating out as its own micro-topic given ODF publishes it separately): donation doesn't require a "perfect" body, is possible after death has already occurred (unlike organ donation), and doesn't preclude any funeral rites. Source: ODF Cornea FAQ and Tissue Bank FAQ (odf.org.za/tissue-bank-faqs/ — page existence confirmed via ODF navigation, not separately fetched in this pass; see Gap G3).

## 2. Current SA donation/waiting-list statistics worth citing

### Primary, dated national dataset — SATS/SATCS report to GODT/WHO-ONT: "5-Year National Organ Transplant Activity, South Africa 2017-2021"
(sats.org.za, published 2024; data collected 2017-2021, waiting-list data first collected for 2021 only)

- **National deceased organ donation rate (per million population):** 1.60 (2017) → 1.59 (2018) → 1.16 (2019) → 0.76 (2020) → **0.48 (2021)** — a steep, sustained decline.
- **National total consented deceased donors:** 91 (2017) → 92 (2018) → 68 (2019) → 45 (2020) → **38 (2021)**.
- **National total solid organ transplants performed (living + deceased donors combined):** 358 (2017) → 391 (2018) → 356 (2019) → 200 (2020) → **229 (2021)**.
- **National transplant waiting list, 31 December 2021 (first year this was collected):** Renal 2,382; Heart 108; Liver 52; Lung 44 — **total 2,586**.
- **National waiting-list mortality, 2021:** Renal 162; Heart 11; Liver 8; Lung 8 deaths.
- The report's own Summary (p.21): "Organ donation and transplantation activity in South Africa is alarmingly low and have deteriorated over the last five years. Activity has dropped during the COVID-19 pandemic and has not since recovered."
- International comparator cited in the same lineage of sources (via a 2020 South African Medical Journal study, referenced in the Spotlight 29 Sep 2025 article): Spain's deceased-donor rate was 47.05 pmp in 2017 (2,183 deceased donors) — roughly 30x South Africa's 2017 rate.

### Supplementary / more recent but secondary-sourced figures (no primary SATS/ODF document with 2022–2026 national data was located in this pass — flagged accordingly)

- **~6,500 South Africans currently need an organ or tissue transplant**, and **only 317 organ transplants were performed nationally in 2024** — reported in allAfrica (27 Aug 2026) and Channel Africa (26 Aug 2026), both attributing figures to the Gauteng Department of Health without a linked primary document. Treat as directionally useful but not verified against a SATS/ODF primary publication.
- **~1,100 Gauteng patients** are awaiting kidney transplants or being assessed for the waitlist — same Gauteng DoH-attributed news coverage, dated Aug 2026.
- **~9,000 people across public and private healthcare were receiving kidney replacement therapy (dialysis)** in South Africa in 2022, per the South African Nephrology Society's Renal Registry (cited via the Spotlight, 29 Sep 2025, article — primary registry document itself not directly located/fetched in this pass).
- ODF's own Statistics page (odf.org.za/statistics/) carries **no independent figures**; it explicitly defers to SATS: "For comprehensive transplant statistics please visit the SATS [website]." This is itself a useful finding — ODF is the awareness/registration body, SATS is the statistics authority, and Beginner Level content should route users to SATS (or a curated stat block sourced from it) rather than to ODF for numbers.
- Spotlight (29 Sep 2025) also independently reports a 2021 SA waiting list of 2,586 (matching the SATS report above) with the same Renal/Heart/Liver/Lung breakdown — cross-confirms the SATS figures as the standing "most recent primary" numbers as of this research date (2026-09-05).

**Note on data quality:** Spotlight explicitly states there is no single, centralised, coordinated database of everyone in South Africa needing a transplant — data is fragmented across provinces/sectors. Limpopo, North West and Mpumalanga are not separately reported in the SATS 5-year report (folded into Gauteng's figures, per a personal communication footnote from the report's compiler). Any stats module for the Beginner Level should state the 2021 figures as "most recent verified national data" rather than implying they are current-year.

## 4. Flagged gaps

- **G1 — Video content unknown.** `The_Journey_of_a_Gift.mp4` (36MB) cannot be transcribed or reviewed by text-based research tooling. Its likely role (awareness/testimonial media) makes it a strong Beginner Level candidate, but someone must actually watch it to confirm: whether it's SA-specific or generic, whether it repeats or contradicts any myth/fact found here, and whether its runtime/format suits a course module. **Needs manual review before it's assigned to a Stage.**
- **G2 — No SA-specific breakdown of religious/cultural objections by community.** The corpus and ODF FAQ both give only a single generic line ("most religions support donation"). South Africa's major faith and cultural communities (e.g., Christian denominations, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, traditional African beliefs) each have distinct, sometimes differing, stances and concerns that a genuinely myth-busting, SA-context Beginner Level would ideally address individually rather than with one blanket reassurance. Neither ODF nor SATS content fetched in this pass provided that granularity. (Note: full multi-language/multi-culture strategy is explicitly out of scope for this map per CONTEXT.md/map.md's "Not yet specified" section — but a plain-English module could still name the major SA faith traditions' documented positions without that being a translation project.)
- **G3 — Tissue Bank FAQ not directly reviewed.** odf.org.za/tissue-bank-faqs/ exists (confirmed via ODF site navigation) but was not fetched in this pass; only the Cornea FAQ was. If tissue donation (skin, bone, heart valves) is to get its own myth-busting content, that page should be read directly before the Beginner Level module list is finalized.
- **G4 — "7 Lives in 7 Steps.pdf" is a mismatch for this Level's stated audience.** The ticket listed it as a Beginner Level candidate, but its actual content is a clinical/hospital-staff decision algorithm (GCS thresholds, brainstem death testing protocol, FPS forms for unnatural deaths, SAPS liaison contacts). This is valuable Intermediate/Advanced-Level or professional-training material, not general-public awareness content. Recommend the module-list ticket either drop it from Beginner Level or use only its title/tagline, and re-file the document itself under Intermediate/Advanced source mapping.
- **G5 — No primary SATS/ODF national dataset newer than 2021.** The most recent verified, primary, national-level donation/waiting-list statistics are for 2021 (published 2024). 2024-2026 figures exist only via news coverage attributing unlinked numbers to the Gauteng Department of Health. If the Curriculum Spec wants a truly "current" headline stat, someone should contact SATS (admin@sats.org.za, per their own report) to check whether a newer national report exists or is in progress, rather than relying on the news-sourced Gauteng-only figures.
- **G6 — Thin raw material for a full myth-busting Stage.** Even combining every source found, the corpus + ODF FAQ content yields roughly 8-10 distinct myths but each is only a sentence or two of grounding text. A genuine Beginner Level Stage will need original narrative/lesson content drafted around these facts — this research surfaces the *material to ground claims in*, not ready-to-use lesson copy (consistent with CONTEXT.md's Curriculum Spec definition explicitly excluding "fully drafted lesson content").
- **G7 — No corpus or ODF/SATS content found addressing some commonly-cited general myths** (outside SA-specific sourcing) such as "you can't donate if you've had cancer/certain illnesses in the past" (partial coverage only — HIV/Hepatitis exclusions are named for cornea donation, but general organ eligibility nuance for past illness isn't addressed anywhere in the reviewed sources) or "living donation is unsafe for the donor." If these are wanted for Beginner Level, they'll need sourcing beyond the current Source Corpus and ODF/SATS site content reviewed here.

## 5. Candidate topic list (for Stage grouping)

Grouped into logical clusters rather than forced into a fixed number — the module-list ticket should decide final Stage count (3-5 per CONTEXT.md).

1. **Why donation matters (the need).** Simplified, non-clinical version of "who needs organs" (Transplant Alchemy 101, Obj. 1) + the "one donor can save 7 lives, tissue can help up to 50 people" framing (7 Lives in 7 Steps tagline; Channel Africa, Gauteng DoH spokesperson quote) + a headline SA shortage stat (2,586 on the waiting list at end-2021; donation rate fallen to 0.48 pmp — SATS 5-Year report). Establishes stakes before myth-busting.
2. **Busting the myths.** The core Beginner Level content: myths #1-#8 and #10 from Section 1 above (disfigurement, cost, illegal sale, religion, age/health eligibility, "registering is enough," family override, tissue-specific concerns). This is the Level's centre of gravity and its best-supported cluster.
3. **How donation actually works (plain-language).** A short, non-clinical overview: organs vs tissue distinction, that the treating team and transplant team are legally separate, that two independent doctors (not on the transplant team) must certify brain death, that it's free and voluntary, and that registering *and* telling your family are both required. Draws lightly from Transplant Alchemy 101 Obj. 3-5 without importing its clinical/legal depth (that belongs to Intermediate Level).
4. **(Optional, pending G1) The human story.** If `The_Journey_of_a_Gift.mp4` turns out (on manual review) to be a testimonial/journey narrative, it could anchor its own short Stage or serve as the closing/emotional-hook segment of Stage 1 or 2 rather than a standalone Stage — decide after watching it.

Three Stages (1-3 above) would satisfy the 3-5 Stage expectation on their own; Stage 4 is contingent on resolving Gap G1.
