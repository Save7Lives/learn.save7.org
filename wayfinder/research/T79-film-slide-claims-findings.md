# T79 The Journey of a Gift: slides 14, 10 and 8

Answers [#79](https://github.com/Save7Lives/learn.save7.org-map/issues/79). Nothing in the course or the film's note was edited. The slide numbers and times are from the #78 inventory.

## Verdicts

1. Slide 14, "A state pathologist (for unnatural deaths) and hospital manager must approve": **not supported by a primary source** as worded. A hospital-level authorisation does exist, but the Act puts it on a medical practitioner, not a manager, and it applies to every recovery, not only unnatural deaths. The course already says this.
2. Slide 10, "No person who fulfils the criteria for brain death has ever developed any return of brain function": **not supported by a primary source** as worded. The guideline says "no documented case", with preconditions and criteria. The slide's version is a flat universal claim.
3. Slide 8, "the irreversible loss of capacity for consciousness and the irreversible loss of capacity to breathe": **agrees with the course**. It is the guideline's definition of death, word for word, and the course uses it. One small flag: the slide heads it "Brain Death".

## 1. "hospital manager must approve" (slide 14, 3:43)

### Verdict

**Not supported by a primary source** as worded. There is a hospital-level authorisation, so the slide is not inventing a step, but the Act and the regulations do not mention a manager, and the slide attaches it to the wrong case.

### Evidence

- **Who authorises a post-mortem or removal of tissue from a body, under the Act.** s 66(2) (Gazette p 68): a post-mortem examination may not take place unless "the medical practitioner in charge of clinical services in the hospital or authorised institution or of the mortuary in question, or any other medical practitioner authorised by such practitioner" has authorised it in writing, or, where there is no such practitioner, "a medical practitioner authorised thereto by the person in charge of such hospital". s 67(2) (Gazette p 69) gives the same person the power to authorise a prescribed institution or person, or an authorised institution, "to remove any specified tissue from the body concerned before burial". "Tissue" in s 1 "includes ... an organ", so this is the section that reaches organ recovery. This is how the course reads it.
- **The role is a medical practitioner, not a manager.** "Person in charge of such hospital" appears only as a fallback, and only for who may authorise a practitioner, never as the one who approves. The words "hospital manager", "manager" and "superintendent" do not appear anywhere in ss 53 to 68 or in R180 or R181 (searched in the extracted text).
- **Not tied to unnatural deaths.** The authorisation in s 67(2) is part of every removal. The slide's sentence ("A state pathologist (for unnatural deaths) and hospital manager must approve") reads as two approvers on the unnatural path.
- **Nothing on forensic authorisation in the Act itself.** s 67(3) (Gazette p 69) concerns a practitioner conducting a post-mortem under s 3 of the Inquests Act 1959 or s 71, who must remove specified tissue if an authorisation exists. s 67(4)(a) bars removal that is "likely to affect the outcome of the examination". There is no "state pathologist" office in the Act or in R180 (no hits for pathologist, forensic, district surgeon).
- **R180 reg 14(1) and (2)** (Gazette pp 82 to 83): no authority for post-mortem or removal of tissue unless the practitioner is satisfied the tissue was donated and the body is not needed for an examination, with a limited exception where the examining practitioner certifies removal will not affect the outcome. R180 reg 9 (p 80) is the two-practitioner death rule. Neither reg names a manager.
- **The one primary document that does use "manager".** Western Cape Circular H 84/2025, section 8 "Obtaining medical manager authorisation" (manual pdf p 12): "The head of the medical institution where the organ recovery is taking place must give authorization for the organ recovery in alignment with the principles of the National Health Act". The same section says the medical manager checks that family consent has been obtained and that forensic authorisation is in place after an unnatural death. This is a provincial policy for Western Cape facilities, not national law, and it says "head of the medical institution" and "medical manager", not "hospital manager".
- **Where the slide's wording probably came from.** The "Transplant Alchemy 101 - Study Guide" (Objective 4) says "Administrative permission to recover organs must be granted by the medical superintendent or hospital manager where the recovery occurs", and the SATS Red File (§3.1, criterion 4) says "medical superintendent or hospital manager". Both are secondary course materials, not primary. "Medical superintendent" is Human Tissue Act language that the course calls out of date; T48 §7.6 already records that the Red File legal summary is not used for legal claims.

### What the course says

- Intermediate, `sa-legal-framework/the-law-and-the-regulations.md` ("The hospital"): recovery needs written authorisation from the medical practitioner in charge of clinical services at the hospital, or a doctor they authorise. "Medical superintendent" is "language from the repealed Human Tissue Act" and out of date. Same in that lesson's study guide and takeaways.
- Advanced, `coordinator-role/the-coordinator-role.md`: the hospital's authorisation, "the Western Cape policy calls this person the *medical manager*".
- Beginner Stage 3 (`how-donation-works/routes-to-donation.md`, `takeaways.md`) names only the Forensic Pathology Service as the extra authority and does not mention a hospital step.
- So the course does not contradict that a hospital authorisation exists. It would contradict the slide's person ("manager") and its pairing with unnatural deaths only.

### Correction row

| The film says | The course says | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Slide 14, Step 3: "A state pathologist (for unnatural deaths) and hospital manager must approve." | The hospital where recovery happens must give written authorisation, from the medical practitioner in charge of clinical services (or a doctor they authorise). After an unnatural death the Forensic Pathology Service must also authorise what may be recovered. | The Act puts the hospital authorisation on a medical practitioner (s 66(2), s 67(2)), applying to every recovery. No Act or regulation names a hospital manager. "Manager" is Western Cape policy language (Circular H 84/2025 s 8). The state pathologist half is already a known correction. |

## 2. The quote (slide 10, 2:37)

### Verdict

**Not supported by a primary source** as worded. No source I could read contains the sentence. The closest source is more careful than the slide, and the course follows the careful version.

### Evidence

- **CCSSA guideline, p 2 (Introduction):** "There is no documented case of a person who fulfils the preconditions and criteria for brain death ever subsequently developing any return of brain function." (refs 2, 3: the AAN 2010 guideline and the ANZICS statement 2008).
- **The slide's sentence** drops "There is no documented case of", drops "preconditions and", drops "subsequently", and turns a statement about what has been recorded into "has ever developed". The word-level overlap ("fulfils the criteria for brain death", "any return of brain function") shows that it is a paraphrase of this sentence. It has no attribution on screen.
- **AAN 2010 (Neurology 74:1911), abstract and Conclusion:** "In adults, there are no published reports of recovery of neurologic function after a diagnosis of brain death using the criteria reviewed in the 1995 American Academy of Neurology practice parameter" and "recovery of neurologic function has not been reported". This too is a statement about reports, limited to adults and to a named set of criteria. It is the source behind the CCSSA sentence, and its wording is not the slide's.
- **Not checked:** the exact text of the ANZICS 2008 statement, the Academy of Medical Royal Colleges code (2008), and the Uniform Determination of Death Act could not be opened here. A web search for the slide's wording found no source containing it. I did not find the sentence quoted in any source, but cannot rule out that one of the three unopened documents holds it.
- The course manual reproduces the CCSSA sentence unchanged (manual pdf p 29).

### What the course says

- Intermediate, `how-donation-happens/determining-death.md`: "there is no documented case of anyone who met the preconditions and criteria for brain death ever regaining any brain function", and the same in `takeaways.md`. The course keeps "no documented case" and the preconditions.
- The replacement film script (T87) already uses "no documented case" and logs the old narration ("no one ... has ever recovered. Ever.") as row 6.
- So the slide is stronger than the course (and the guideline), and an "ever" highlighted on screen makes it stronger still. This is a gap in strength, not a different fact, but a Stage Quiz or a sceptical reader could be given a universal claim the sources do not make.

### Correction row

| The film says | The course says | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Slide 10, quote with "ever" and "any" highlighted: "No person who fulfils the criteria for brain death has ever developed any return of brain function." | There is no documented case of anyone who met the preconditions and criteria for brain death regaining any brain function. | The CCSSA guideline (p 2) and the AAN 2010 guideline state that no case has been documented or reported, with the preconditions and criteria. The slide states it as an absolute, shows it as a quotation, and has no attribution. |

## 3. The brain-death definition (slide 8, 1:58)

### Verdict

**Agrees with the course.** The slide's wording is the guideline's definition of death, and the course uses it. The narration's different wording is the narration's issue and already covered (T87 row 5), and it is also consistent with the course.

### Evidence

- **CCSSA guideline, p 5 ("General statements")**: "Death is the clinical point of irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness and the irreversible loss of the capacity to breathe." (ref 16, Gardiner, Shemie, Manara and Opdam, Br J Anaesth 2012). The slide's text is this sentence with the articles removed ("the irreversible loss of capacity for consciousness and the irreversible loss of capacity to breathe"). The same page says death is determined "by either neurological or circulatory criteria".
- **The Act, s 1 (Gazette p 10):** "'death' means brain death". The Act has one definition of death, and it is brain death, with no wording about consciousness or breathing. The regulation that sets who establishes death, R180 reg 9 (Gazette p 80), does not define it either, and no definition of brain death turned up in R180 or R181. The Draft Regulations on Organ Transplantation (2026) define a "deceased donor" as diagnosed with brain death or circulatory death (T48 §1).
- **Which formulation the SA sources use:** the guideline uses the consciousness-and-breathing definition for death in general, and describes brain death as the neurological route to it. The Act and regulations do not use either of the two sentences. The narration's "complete ... end of all brain function" matches the guideline's preconditions wording ("complete and irreversible loss of all brain function" is in the guideline's brain-death preconditions as T48 §3.1 records).
- **One flag.** The slide heads the sentence "Brain Death". In the guideline it is the definition of death itself, reached by either neurological or circulatory criteria, and a reader could take it as the definition of brain death only. The course keeps them apart.

### What the course says

- Intermediate `how-donation-happens/intro.md`: "Death is the irreversible loss of the capacity for consciousness together with the irreversible loss of the capacity to breathe." Same in `takeaways.md` and `study-guide.md`, and `determining-death.md` says brain death and circulatory death are "two routes to establishing the same fact: the capacity for consciousness and the capacity to breathe have both been lost, irreversibly".
- The same file defines brain death as "the complete and irreversible loss of all brain function, including the brainstem".
- Intermediate `sa-legal-framework/the-law-and-the-regulations.md`: "'Death' means brain death. That is the Act's own definition in section 1."

### Correction row

None needed for the wording. Optional, if the registry wants the label fixed:

| The film says | The course says | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Slide 8 is headed "Brain Death" over the definition "irreversible loss of capacity for consciousness and ... capacity to breathe". | That sentence defines death. Brain death is the neurological route to it: the complete and irreversible loss of all brain function, including the brainstem. | CCSSA p 5 gives the sentence as the definition of death, reached "by either neurological or circulatory criteria". Low priority: the wording is right, the heading is loose. |
